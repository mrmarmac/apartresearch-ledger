A model paired the two texts as the same commitment in two consecutive versions of the Anthropic Responsible Scaling Policy. Decide the change to the obligation, not to the words.

  • Kept: no material change. Rewording, renumbering and new cross-references are not material.
  • Strengthened: one or more dimensions move to a larger obligation, and no dimension moves the other way.
  • Weakened: one or more dimensions move to a smaller obligation. If a change is stronger in one dimension and weaker in a different dimension, select Weakened.
  • Not the same thing: the later passage is not the counterpart of the earlier commitment.
  • Cannot tell: the text is cut by a page break or mixed with other text, and the context does not make it clear.
  • Extract length is not a change. The later text can be a shorter or longer extract, because of page breaks. Before you decide that words were removed, open "Text around the later passage".

The six dimensions. A change is material if it changes one of these:

  1. Scope: which capabilities, risk domains or models are covered.
  2. Threshold or trigger: the level, time or condition that requires action.
  3. Actor: who does it, verifies it, or gets access (a third party added or removed).
  4. Obligation strength: must / will / commit to > intend to / aim to / expect to > may / could / consider > recommend / encourage.
  5. Disclosure: what is published, to whom, when, with what redaction.
  6. Consequence: what action a result requires, and when that action can be deferred or overridden.

Three special rules.

  • Present tense is equal to "will". "We conduct X" to "we will conduct X" is not material. "We conduct X" to "we may conduct X" is material.
  • A list introduced by "including" or "consists of" defines scope. To shorten it is material. A list introduced by "e.g." or "such as" is an example. To change it is not material.
  • A wider condition for suspending a commitment is Weakened, even if the core commitment is the same.
