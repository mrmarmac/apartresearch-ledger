How to read a status

= KeptA counterpart exists and nothing material changed. The words can differ.
↑ StrengthenedThe obligation became larger in one or more ways, and smaller in none.
↓ WeakenedThe obligation became smaller in one or more ways. Also used for mixed changes.
× RemovedNo counterpart in this version.
→ RelocatedThe counterpart moved to a companion document or a non-binding section.
+ AddedIn this version, with no counterpart in the version before.
• First seenThe first version where the Ledger sees the commitment. No claim of change.

A status describes the change from the version before. Rules: definitions.

Read this before you cite a status

  • Statuses are a reference, not ground truth. Most come from the coding of Zhu (2026): a language model first pass, adjudicated by that study's author. No second person coded independently.
  • The Ledger traced 7 version pairs itself, with text matching and a language model. 84 entries were coded by the model. One person checked 73 of them.
  • "In force" means "in the last traced version". It says nothing about whether the developer complies.
  • "Not traced" is not a removal. The record stops because the Ledger could not follow the commitment further.
