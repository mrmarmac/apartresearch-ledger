Weakened 1

The obligation became smaller in one or more ways. Also used for mixed changes.

↓ Weakenedxai-mitigation-f2dcMitigations
Under this RMF, FAIF, xAI’s models apply heightened safeguards if they receive user prompts that pose a foreseeable and non-trivial risk of resulting in large-scale violence, terrorism, or the use, development, or proliferation of weapons of mass destruction, including CBRN weapons, and major cyber attacks on critical infrastructure. ... In this RMF, FAIF, we particularly focus on requests that pose a foreseeable and non-trivial risk of more than one hundred deaths or over $1 billion in damages Catastrophic Risk.

Mixed change. Stronger in one way and weaker in another. The status is then Weakened.

Why this status (source coding): The focus threshold 'more than one hundred deaths or over $1 billion in damages from weapons of mass destruction or cyberterrorist attacks on critical infrastructure' becomes 'a Catastrophic Risk', defined in footnote 1 by the TFAIA as death or serious injury to 'more than 50 people or more than one billion dollars' from a single incident involving expert-level CBRN assistance, an autonomous cyberattack or crime with 'no meaningful human oversight', or evading control. Both directions: the casualty anchor falls from 100 to 50 (S dim 2); the covered conduct is redrawn (cyberattacks only when carried out without human oversight, CBRN assistance qualified as expert-level, evasion of control added), which is a change of scope (dim 1). Coded W per codebook section 4.

Why this status (source coding): The consequence rule ('may depend on how a model performs on relevant benchmarks') and the benefit-outweighs-risk override are unchanged; the framework-update clause gains two named triggers: 'before major new capabilities are launched, and in response to incidents'. Coded S on threshold/trigger.

↑ Strengthenedxai-security-adf9Security
xAI has implemented appropriate information security standards sufficient to prevent its critical model information from being stolen by a motivated non-state actor. Practices include encryption of model weights, role-based access controls, and real-time monitoring to prevent unauthorized transfer. To prevent the unauthorized proliferation of advanced AI systems, we also implement security measures against the large-scale extraction and distillation of reasoning traces, which have been shown to be highly effective in quickly reproducing advanced capabilities while expending far fewer computational resources than the original AI system10. ...

Why this status (source coding): A sentence is added naming the practices: 'Practices include encryption of model weights, role-based access controls, and real-time monitoring to prevent unauthorized transfer'; the security commitment gains specified content (increase in specificity, the mirror image of calibration example G).
