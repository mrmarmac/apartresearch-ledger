What it is

AI developers publish safety frameworks: documents that set capability thresholds, the evaluations used to test against them, and what the developer will do when a threshold is crossed. Developers revise these documents. The Ledger follows each commitment through those revisions.

A commitment is a statement in a safety framework in which the developer commits itself to a practice. Admissions and descriptions are not commitments, and are not in the Ledger.

The Ledger holds 447 commitments from 5 developers across 23 framework versions, with 478 recorded changes. It does not code the frameworks afresh. The seed data is the coding of the Frontier Safety Framework Corpus (Zhu, 2026, Silent Revision). That study gives a commitment a new row in each pair of versions. The Ledger connects those rows into one history and gives it one ID.

Identifiers

A commitment ID has the form developer-topic-hash, for example xai-governance-673f.

  • The developer part does not change when a company changes its name.
  • The topic is the topic at first appearance.
  • The hash is the first 4 hexadecimal characters of the SHA-256 of the developer, the first version and the normalised first quote. If two IDs collide, the later one takes one more character.
  • The ID is assigned at first appearance. A later change of wording or topic does not change it. An ID is never reused.
  • When two histories are joined, the older ID continues. The other becomes a former ID, and its page points to the new one. 14 histories were joined this way on 15 November 2026.

Each history entry also has an ID: commitment-id@version-id. It is the identifier of the matching item in the RSS feed.

Statuses

A status on a history entry describes the change from the version before.

StatusMeaning
• First seenThe first version where the Ledger sees the commitment. No claim of change.
+ AddedIn this version, with no counterpart in the version before.
= KeptA counterpart exists and nothing material changed. The words can differ.
↑ StrengthenedThe obligation became larger in one or more ways, and smaller in none.
↓ WeakenedThe obligation became smaller in one or more ways. Also used for mixed changes.
× RemovedNo counterpart in this version.
→ RelocatedThe counterpart moved to a companion document or a non-binding section.

The source study judges a change on six dimensions: scope, threshold or trigger, actor, obligation strength, disclosure scope, and consequence. "Relocated" does not mean "moved to a different section" of the same document.

The developer's own account

For each material change, the source study records whether the developer's own account of the revision identifies it.

LabelMeaning
Identified in the developer's accountThe developer's account identifies this change.
Area identified, change not identifiedThe developer's account identifies the area, but not the change.
Not identified in the developer's accountThe developer published an account. It does not identify this change.
No account publishedThe developer published no account of this revision.

Who decided each entry

The Ledger has 1,533 history entries. Each one says who decided it.

Decided byEntriesWhat that means
Source coding877The coding of the Silent Revision study (Zhu, 2026): a first pass by a Claude model, adjudicated by that study's author. No independent human coder.
Text match572The same words are in this version. The status is Kept. No judgement by a person or a model.
Language model84A language model found the counterpart and coded the change, in two runs. One person checked 73 of these entries. Each entry says whether it was checked.

Histories are connected across versions by text: a note in the source data that names the earlier row, then identical text after normalisation, then a similarity ratio of 0.9 or more. For the version pairs that the Ledger traced itself, a language model found the counterpart. A commitment that was fully rewritten gets a new ID.

The model tests are not independent of the source coding. Both sides used Claude models, so agreement between them shows consistency within one model family.

Known limits

  • The statuses are not ground truth. A Claude model made the source study's first pass and that study's author adjudicated it. No other person coded independently.
  • One person checked the Ledger's own tracing. That person is the Ledger's author. For Meta v2.1 he saw the model's answers first.
  • Tracing is weaker at a rewrite. The model found the source's counterpart in 20 of 20 cases on an incremental revision, and in 10 of 20 on a full rewrite.
  • New commitments can be missed. The step that looks for them found 26 of 34 in a test. For Anthropic v2.1, v2.2 and v3.1 to v3.4 the Ledger does not look for new commitments at all.
  • 62 histories stop early with the status "Not traced". 25 of them overlap a later history that could not be joined one to one, so some commitments may be counted twice.
  • "In force" means "in the last traced version". A new version starts to count only after someone traces it. A daily watcher reports when a framework file or page changes.
  • Frameworks only. Model cards, usage policies and violations are not in the Ledger. Watchtower covers those.
  • Tags are keyword rules. They can miss or over-match.
  • 16 quotes were not found by the automatic check against the text extracted from the source file. Each entry says whether its quote was found.

Reuse

  • Data: ledger.json, described by schema.json and SCHEMA.md. Licence: CC BY 4.0. Credit this project and the source corpus.
  • Feed: feed.xml, one item for each recorded change.
  • Code: GitHub, MIT licence. The site and the feed are built from ledger.json with no language model step.
  • Quotes from framework documents belong to their publishers. The Ledger links to each document by address and SHA-256.

Source corpus: Zhu, Louis Yiven (2026). Silent Revision: a versioned corpus of frontier AI safety frameworks and their revision accounts. Zenodo. arXiv:2609.08789. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22670700.

The Ledger builds on Watchtower by The Midas Project and extends the idea to a record for each commitment. The Midas Project did not take part in this work and has not endorsed it. Made for the Apart Research AI x Epistemics sprint, November 2026.
